## Material 2 — Careers page

**The network**
Tidewater maintains 3,800 km of overhead line and 410 substations serving 140,000 homes across 26 inhabited islands and the northern coast. Last winter our crews restored 94% of storm-related outages within 24 hours (from our published annual network performance statement).

**What working here is like**

*Consequential*
Most of our customers have no alternative supply. When a line goes down on an island, our crew is the only way the power comes back.

*Planned, not improvised*
Every field crew has a dedicated planner who prepares access, permits, materials and ferry bookings before the crew leaves the depot. Standard crew size is four. We do not allow lone working on live equipment — ever.

*Built from within*
Our technician apprenticeship lasts three years and is paid from day one. Of the 18 apprentices who started in 2022, 16 completed. 11 of our 14 current field supervisors started with us as apprentices.

**What this job is not**
We'd rather you know now:
- You'll be on call one week in five. On-call weeks are paid at a flat supplement plus overtime for call-outs.
- Winter storms mean extended shifts — last season, crews worked an average of six shifts over 12 hours.
- Island work means ferries and sometimes overnight stays. Travel time is paid.
- If you want a predictable, indoor, nine-to-five role, this isn't it.

**Our approach to safety**
We believe we have a world-class safety culture. Every crew holds a pre-job briefing, and any crew member can stop a job without needing a manager's approval.

**Software at Tidewater**
A nine-person team builds the outage-prediction and crew-dispatch tools our technicians use, including at 3 a.m. in a storm. Technicians sit in on every sprint review.

## Material 3 — Job advertisement: Line Technician (Field)

**Line Technician — Northern Isles Depot**

**Salary:** £36,500–£41,000 depending on qualifications, plus on-call supplement and overtime
**Pattern:** Monday–Friday days, on call one week in five
**Base:** Northern Isles depot; regular travel by ferry to island sites, travel time paid

**What you'll do**
Maintain and repair overhead lines and pole-mounted equipment on the islands and northern coast. Most days you'll be on routine maintenance with your crew of four; in storm season you'll be on fault restoration, often in wind and rain.

**Your first 90 days**
Weeks 1–2: safety and network induction at the depot. Weeks 3–12: working alongside an experienced crew, with sign-offs on each equipment type before you work on it without direct supervision.

**You'll need**
- A recognised overhead-line qualification, or an electrical qualification and willingness to complete our conversion course
- A full driving licence
- Comfort working at height and outdoors in all weather

**Not required:** previous island or utility experience.

**How we hire**
1. Online application (about 10 minutes, no cover letter)
2. 30-minute phone call with a crew supervisor
3. Practical assessment at the depot — we show you the tasks in advance
4. Offer, usually within 3 weeks of applying

Questions before applying? Call Morag, our field recruitment lead, on the number on our careers page — she's a former line technician.

**Apply now**

## Material 4 — Job advertisement: Software Engineer (Network Tools)

**Software Engineer — Network Tools**

**Salary:** £52,000–£62,000
**Work mode:** Hybrid, two days a week in the Harbourside office

You'll join a nine-person team building the outage-prediction and crew-dispatch tools that our field crews rely on. You'll work on our dispatch platform, our outage-prediction models and the mobile app technicians use on site.

Technicians sit in on every sprint review, so you'll hear directly whether what you built worked in the field. Engineers join one field shadowing day per quarter.

The team shares an on-call rota for the dispatch system during storm season; on-call is compensated.

We're a growing team with exciting challenges and great opportunities for career development.

## Material 5 — Employee story

**"I nearly quit in February."**
*Callum, Line Technician, joined as an apprentice in 2021*

"The first winter is hard, and I won't pretend otherwise. We had eleven days of storms in a row and I nearly quit in February. What kept me was that Ewan, my crew lead, never sent us out without a plan. Every morning we knew where we were going, what we'd find and what we'd need. That's what makes the hard days doable.

Three years later I'm signed off on everything we work on and I'm mentoring this year's apprentices. I still don't love February."
