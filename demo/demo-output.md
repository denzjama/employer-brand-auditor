## 2. WHAT'S WORKING

- **The engineer ad gives the decision facts.** It states the salary range (£38,000–£44,000 plus on-call allowance), 40 hours Monday to Friday plus on-call one week in four, field-based work with a company van, a patch of two hospitals and more than 60 residential blocks, solo routine visits and paired major repairs, and a reply target of 10 working days. You can picture a week in this job from the ad alone.
- **Ilse Varga's story is the most credible item in the set.** It is attributed (apprentice from 2019, qualified 2023) and describes a concrete event: a 2am call to Westmere General with a patient trolley in a stuck lift. It admits difficulty, and its headline is itself the admission: "The first winter on call nearly put me off." It also shows a practice in action: her mentor talked her through the call by phone, and this year she started mentoring two apprentices herself.
- **The Matter pillar puts evidence directly under the claim.** It gives 11,200 call-outs and 92% of hospital entrapment calls reached within 45 minutes, with a timeframe ("Last year"). The careers-page sentence "When a lift stops in a 14-storey residential block, our engineers are the people who get residents home." is the one line in the brand layer that says what the work means for the person doing it.
- **The Master pillar names a mechanism.** Apprentices are paired with a senior engineer for the whole programme and work towards a named qualification level.

## 3. BIGGEST PROBLEMS

**1. HIGH — The careers page repeats the pillar structure but drops the evidence.**
The "Why join us?" list follows the three pillars almost one-for-one: work that matters, training from day one, a supportive close-knit team. But each bullet is a lexicon phrase. The figures behind Matter and the apprentice pairing behind Master stay in the brand guide. The promise line "Our promise: Lift people up." appears in none of the other four touchpoints.

The evidence exists, but it isn't in the careers page's "Why join us?" list. As written, the page's reasons to join contain no employer-specific information. Its second paragraph ("We offer competitive pay, great benefits and real opportunities to grow." and "Our people are at the heart of everything we do.") is built entirely from lexicon phrases.

**2. HIGH — The CSC ad has no pay figure and no employer-specific offer.**
The ad gives "Competitive salary" where the engineer ad gives a range. Its opening ("Be part of a dynamic team at the heart of our operation!") and its "A friendly, supportive team" contain no employer-specific information. The ad does have concrete tasks: logging faults, dispatching engineers, and keeping customers updated until their lift is back in service. That is a direct link to the Matter idea, but the ad doesn't make it. The ad also doesn't give weekly hours or how Saturday shifts are allocated within the 7am–7pm Monday-to-Saturday rota.

**3. HIGH — "Flexible working" is undefined, and it's attached to the role whose stated terms show no flexibility.**
The careers page and the engineer ad both list "Flexible working". The engineer ad gives no detail on it, and its stated terms are fixed: 40 hours Monday to Friday, on-call one week in four, field-based. The only concrete flexibility in the set is the CSC hybrid arrangement (two days a week at home after training), and that ad doesn't call it flexible working. So the label appears where nothing is defined, and the one defined arrangement doesn't carry the label.

This isn't a stated contradiction, so I haven't marked it CONFLICTING. It is still an unexplained tension in the engineer ad.

**4. MEDIUM — The Belong pillar is adjectives, and its only evidence is one engineer's account.**
"We look out for each other." and "Like a family, we celebrate wins together." describe no practice. The only support for team culture anywhere in the set is Ilse's mentor talking her through a call. That is one person's experience in engineering, and it says nothing about the CSC team that the CSC ad calls supportive.

**5. MEDIUM — The development promise covers apprentices; experienced hires and CSC staff get only a phrase.**
The Master pillar's mechanism applies to apprentices. The engineer ad requires NVQ Level 3 or equivalent, which suggests (INFERENCE) it targets already-qualified engineers, and it offers only "Training and development opportunities". The CSC ad mentions training only as a condition for hybrid working. No apprentice ad was supplied.

**6. MEDIUM — Unsupported status and reward claims.**
"We are proud to be an employer of choice for engineering talent." has no source, scope or criteria anywhere in the material. "Competitive" pay appears twice with no comparison. In the CSC ad it appears with no figure at all.

**7. LOW — Generic CTAs outside the engineer ad.**
"Apply today and take the next step in your career." and "Apply now through our careers page." say nothing about what to send, who to contact or what happens next.

## 4. GENERICITY TEST

**CLAIM:** "Our promise: Lift people up."
- **VERDICT:** GENERIC
- **GENERICITY RISK:** MEDIUM
- **WHY:** The wordplay on lifts is category-specific, not employer-specific, so a comparable lift-maintenance employer could reuse it unchanged. The line doesn't say who is lifted (residents and patients, or employees) or what the employee gets.
  - Substitution: survives
  - Ubiquity: rare (no lexicon terms)
  - Specificity: none
  - Defensibility: unowned
- **WHAT WOULD MAKE IT OWNABLE:** Attach it to something only Quellmoor can state, such as the Westmere portfolio or the hospital response figures, and say what it means for the person doing the work. Otherwise it remains a tagline, not the proposition.

**CLAIM (Matter):** "Our work keeps hospitals, homes and stations accessible. Last year our engineers answered 11,200 call-outs, and 92% of entrapment calls in hospitals were reached within 45 minutes."
- **VERDICT:** PARTIALLY DIFFERENTIATED
- **GENERICITY RISK:** LOW–MEDIUM
- **WHY:** The first sentence alone survives substitution, but the figures break it.
  - Substitution: breaks (on the figures)
  - Ubiquity: common ("Matter" follows the meaningful-work pattern)
  - Specificity: high (numbers, scope, timeframe)
  - Defensibility: supportable. The portfolio is structural, but the evidence is a last-year service metric, and the pillar doesn't say what it means for the employee. The careers-page sentence about engineers getting residents home does, but it sits in a different touchpoint.
- **WHAT WOULD MAKE IT OWNABLE:** Put the employee consequence next to the figures.
  - EVIDENCE NEEDED: whether the figures continue in the current period.

**CLAIM (Master your trade):** "Every apprentice is paired with a senior engineer for the whole programme and works towards a Level 3 lift engineering qualification."
- **VERDICT:** PARTIALLY DIFFERENTIATED
- **GENERICITY RISK:** MEDIUM
- **WHY:** Another employer could adopt and publish the same practice, but it would have to bear the cost of senior-engineer time.
  - Substitution: bends
  - Ubiquity: common (the pillar's next sentence, "employer of choice", is a lexicon phrase)
  - Specificity: some to high (a named practice, its duration and the qualification level)
  - Defensibility: supportable
  - Scope: apprentices only
- **WHAT WOULD MAKE IT OWNABLE:** Participation and outcomes.
  - EVIDENCE NEEDED: how many apprentices are in the programme, completion numbers, and what development qualified engineers and CSC staff receive.

**CLAIM (Belong):** "We look out for each other. Like a family, we celebrate wins together."
- **VERDICT:** GENERIC
- **GENERICITY RISK:** HIGH
- **WHY:** No employer-specific information.
  - Substitution: survives
  - Ubiquity: ubiquitous ("like a family", "look out for each other")
  - Specificity: none
  - Defensibility: unowned
- **WHAT WOULD MAKE IT OWNABLE:** Replace the adjectives with practices.
  - EVIDENCE NEEDED: what support an engineer on call or working alone can draw on, and what "celebrate wins" refers to, if anything.

**CLAIM (Flexible working, careers page and engineer ad):** "Flexible working"
- **VERDICT:** GENERIC
- **GENERICITY RISK:** HIGH
- **WHY:** The phrase has no content in either touchpoint that uses it.
  - Substitution: survives
  - Ubiquity: common
  - Specificity: none
  - Defensibility: unowned
- **WHAT WOULD MAKE IT OWNABLE:** State what is flexible, for which role, and under which conditions, or remove it.

## 6. RED FLAGS

**RF01 Generic employer claim — HIGH**
- **Where:** Careers page, "Why join us?" list and second paragraph.
- **Evidence:** "We offer competitive pay, great benefits and real opportunities to grow." "Our people are at the heart of everything we do." The bullets "A supportive, close-knit team" and "Flexible working".
- **Why it matters:** These are the page's stated reasons to join, and none contains employer-specific information. The specific material that would differentiate them (Matter figures, apprentice pairing) sits in the brand guide.
- **Fix:** Move the evidence into the list (see BEFORE → AFTER).

**RF06 Benefit without proof — HIGH**
- **Where:** Careers page; Engineer ad, "What we offer".
- **Evidence:** "Flexible working", with no description, listed alongside 40 fixed weekday hours, field-based work and on-call one week in four.
- **Why it matters:** The engineer ad, which offers this benefit, doesn't say what it is.

**RF16 Great-team cliché — MEDIUM**

- **Evidence:** "Like a family, we celebrate wins together." "A friendly, supportive team"
- **Why it matters:** The material contains no information about how teams support each other, apart from one engineer's account.

**RF14 Vague development promise — MEDIUM**

**RF02 Unsupported superlative — MEDIUM**

## 7. TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Rebuild the careers page "Why join us?" around the evidence you already have.**
   - **What:** Put the Matter figures (keeping "last year") and the apprentice pairing practice into the list. Link to Ilse Varga's story. Cut the reward and "people at the heart" lines.
   - **Why first:** It's the cheapest fix with the largest effect on Proof, Differentiation and Clarity, and it needs no new facts.
   - **Evidence needed:** whether the call-out and response figures continue in the current period.

2. **Make the terms honest per role.**
   - **What:** Remove "Flexible working" from the engineer ad unless you can say what it means. Add a pay figure to the CSC ad and state its hours. Drop "competitive" wherever no comparison is shown.
   - **Why second:** These are decision facts. Right now the engineer ad promises something its own terms don't show, and the CSC ad asks for an application without stating pay.
   - **Evidence needed:** CSC salary; what flexible working means and for whom; CSC weekly hours and Saturday allocation; the on-call allowance amount.

3. **Make on-call reality part of the proposition, and replace Belong's adjectives with practices.**
   - **What:** The engineer ad states the trade-off (on-call one week in four, mostly solo visits), and Ilse's story shows it handled. The careers page and proposition leave both out. Lead with them.
   - **Why third:** It needs some new input (practices, further stories), but it's the one idea in the set that couldn't be reused unchanged by a comparable employer that doesn't run the same rota.
   - **Evidence needed:** what support exists for an engineer on call or working alone; further attributable stories, including one from the CSC team. Use real employee input; don't draft quotes.

## 8. BEFORE → AFTER

### 1. Careers page, "Why join us?"

**Original:**
> We offer competitive pay, great benefits and real opportunities to grow. Our people are at the heart of everything we do.
> Why join us? Work that matters to the communities we serve / Training and development from day one / A supportive, close-knit team / Flexible working

**Problem:** Every reason is a lexicon phrase. The evidence for two of them sits in the brand guide.

**Recommended logic:** One bullet per supported pillar, with its evidence attached. Role-scoped terms. Leave out what can't be shown yet.

**Rewrite:**
> **Why join us?**
> - **Work people depend on.** We maintain around 3,400 lifts and escalators in hospitals, housing blocks, shopping centres and railway stations across the Westmere region. Last year our engineers answered 11,200 call-outs, and 92% of entrapment calls in hospitals were reached within 45 minutes.

- EVIDENCE NEEDED: what flexible working means, for which roles.
- CONFIRM: whether the last-year figures reflect current performance; if you publish newer ones, state their year.

### 2. Engineer ad, "What we offer"

**Original:** Flexible working / 25 days' holiday plus bank holidays / Pension and health cash plan / Training and development opportunities

**Problem:** Two of the four items have no content, and one of them sits uneasily with the ad's own terms. The ad's strongest content (the rota, solo work, the patch) appears higher up but is never presented as part of the offer.

> **What we offer**
> - £38,000–£44,000 plus on-call allowance
> - 25 days' holiday plus bank holidays
> - Pension and health cash plan
>
> **What to expect**
> - 40 hours a week, Monday to Friday, plus on-call one week in four.

> - You will work alone on most routine visits and in pairs for major repairs.
> - Ilse Varga, Lift Service Engineer: "The first winter on call was hard." Read her story.

- EVIDENCE NEEDED: the on-call allowance amount.
- EVIDENCE NEEDED: what support an engineer has on call.
- EVIDENCE NEEDED: what training qualified engineers receive.
- EVIDENCE NEEDED: what flexible working means for this role, if anything.

### 3. Proposition, Belong pillar

**Problem:** It's adjectives only, and the one supporting practice in the set (a mentor's phone support) isn't used.

> **Belong.** During Ilse Varga's first winter on call, a 2am call took her to a lift stuck between floors at Westmere General with a patient trolley inside. Her mentor, Joe, talked her through it on the phone. She qualified in 2023, and has started mentoring two apprentices herself.

- CONFIRM: the year Ilse started mentoring (her story says "this year").
- EVIDENCE NEEDED: practices beyond this single account, including any for the CSC team.
- CONFIRM: whether "celebrate wins" refers to an actual practice.

The rewrite paraphrases Ilse's own story. It is not a new quote.
